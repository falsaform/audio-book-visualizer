Chapter 1

Call me Ishmael. Some years ago, having little or no money in my purse, I
thought I would sail about a little and see the watery part of the world. It
was a way I had of driving off the spleen. Ishmael was a young man, lean and
restless, with dark windblown hair and the weathered hands of a sailor.

Whenever I find myself growing grim about the mouth, I account it high time to
get to sea as soon as I can. The harbor of New Bedford lay grey under a cold
November dawn, its docks crowded with masts and the cries of gulls.

Chapter 2

There now is your insular city of the Manhattoes, belted round by wharves as
Indian isles by coral reefs. Captain Ahab strode the quarterdeck, a tall and
grizzled man with a livid white scar running down his weathered face, one leg
of carved whalebone planted firmly on the boards. His eyes burned with a fixed
and terrible purpose.

The Pequod set sail at last upon a green and heaving sea, her sails full
against a sky of torn clouds, while the crew sang low at the capstan and the
spray flew silver from her bow.
